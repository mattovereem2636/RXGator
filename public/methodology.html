How RXGator Works: Data Sources and Methodology

The short version: RXGator searches 22 data sources at once and ranks results from lowest price to highest. It is free for consumers today. Affiliate relationships, sponsorships, and partnerships do not change the ranking order.

The 22 Data Sources

RXGator groups its sources into three kinds. Government benchmarks show what pharmacies pay or what programs allow. They are not always prices you can get at the counter.

How Fresh Is the Data?

  • Cost Plus Drugs, Medicare Part D, RxNorm, MedlinePlus, openFDA — queried in real time on every search.
  • NADAC — CMS publishes new NADAC data weekly. RXGator shows the benchmark from its most recent update.
  • GoodRx and SingleCare — cached data, updated periodically. Prices are recent but may be hours or days old.
  • Walmart, Costco, Amazon RxPass — built-in lists, updated when program pricing changes (typically quarterly).
  • Federal Upper Limit — updated monthly from CMS data.
  • All other sources — refreshed on their own schedules. Cached sources can be hours or days old.

See the FAQ for the full answer to "How often are prices updated?"

What the Prices Do and Do Not Mean

  • Costco figures are estimates based on published reporting, not quotes from a store.
  • Discount card prices change by pharmacy, location, and day. Your price at the counter can differ.
  • Benchmarks such as NADAC, Federal Upper Limit, VA Federal Supply Schedule, and Texas WAC show costs inside the supply chain. They are not retail prices.
  • Always verify the final price at the pharmacy counter.

How RXGator Is Funded

RXGator is funded by community support, advertising, and affiliate commissions. Affiliate commissions never change how results are ranked. The FAQ explains each one.
