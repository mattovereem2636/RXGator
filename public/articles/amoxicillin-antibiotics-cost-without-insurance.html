How Much Does Amoxicillin Cost Without Insurance in 2026?

A typical course of generic amoxicillin costs about $8 to $18 without insurance. Here is what different forms cost, why prices vary, and how to use antibiotics safely so you only pay once.

The Short Answer

Amoxicillin is one of the most prescribed antibiotics in the United States, and it is cheap as a generic. A typical 7-day course of 500 mg capsules (21 capsules) costs about $13.64 at average retail prices, according to GoodRx. With a free discount coupon, the same prescription can cost as little as $8.19. Most cash buyers pay between $8 and $18 for a full course.

That price range makes amoxicillin one of the few drugs where a coupon often beats an insurance copay. If your plan charges a $15 or $20 copay for generics, ask the pharmacist for the cash price with a discount card before you pay.

Why the Price Can Still Surprise You

Generic amoxicillin is inexpensive to make. The retail price at the counter still varies, for three main reasons.

  • Each pharmacy sets its own cash price. Large chains often post a high "usual and customary" price. A discount card or a different store can cut that price in half.
  • Combination drugs cost more. Amoxicillin with clavulanate (brand name Augmentin) treats some infections that plain amoxicillin cannot. The combination usually costs more than plain amoxicillin. Ask your prescriber whether plain amoxicillin will work for your infection.
  • Liquid forms need mixing and refrigeration. The pharmacy mixes liquid amoxicillin when you pick it up. The mixed liquid lasts about 14 days, so you cannot buy it far ahead.

Five Ways to Pay Less

  1. Compare prices before you fill. Ask the prescriber to send the prescription after you choose a pharmacy. RxGator shows prices from 21 sources in one search.
  2. Ask for the cash price with a coupon. For a short antibiotic course, the coupon price can be lower than your copay. The pharmacy cannot apply both, so ask for the lower one.
  3. Ask about store programs. Some grocery-store pharmacies have run free or low-cost generic antibiotic programs. These programs change often, so call and ask.
  4. Check warehouse club pharmacies. Costco and Sam's Club pharmacies often post low cash prices on common generics.
  5. Use a telehealth visit for simple infections. A video visit can cost less than an urgent care visit. Telehealth suits some infections, but not all. A clinician may still need to examine you in person.

Use Antibiotics Safely So You Only Pay Once

The cheapest antibiotic course is the one that works the first time. These safety steps protect your health and your wallet.

Antibiotics Do Not Treat Viruses

Colds, most sore throats, the flu, and COVID come from viruses. Amoxicillin does not treat viral infections. An antibiotic you do not need still costs money, can cause side effects, and helps germs become resistant. If your doctor says you do not need an antibiotic, that advice saves you money too.

Take the Full Course as Directed

Take amoxicillin exactly as your prescriber tells you. Do not stop early because you feel better unless your prescriber tells you to stop. An infection that comes back can mean a second visit and a second prescription.

Do Not Use Leftover or Shared Antibiotics

Leftover pills from an old prescription may be the wrong drug, the wrong dose, or too few doses for a new infection. Never take antibiotics prescribed for someone else. If you have leftover antibiotics, dispose of them safely at a pharmacy drop-off kiosk.

Tell Your Prescriber About Allergies

Amoxicillin belongs to the penicillin family. If you had a reaction to penicillin in the past, tell your prescriber and pharmacist before you take it. Seek emergency care right away for trouble breathing, swelling of the face or throat, or a widespread rash.

Ask About Interactions

Amoxicillin can interact with some other medicines, including certain blood thinners. Give your pharmacist a full list of your medicines and supplements. Ask whether you need any extra monitoring while you take the antibiotic.

Amoxicillin for Children

Children often get the liquid form. The dose depends on the child's weight, so the bottle size and price can differ from child to child. Ask the pharmacist how to store the liquid and how to measure each dose. Use the dosing syringe or cup from the pharmacy, not a kitchen spoon.

Children 18 and younger may qualify for Medicaid or CHIP, which cover antibiotics with little or no cost. If your child is uninsured, ask about these programs at the doctor's office or pharmacy.

Other Common Antibiotics

Amoxicillin is not the right drug for every infection. Your prescriber may choose a different antibiotic based on the type of infection, your allergies, and local resistance patterns. Common alternatives include azithromycin, cephalexin, doxycycline, and amoxicillin with clavulanate. Most of these are also available as generics.

Prices for these generics vary more than amoxicillin prices do. A short course of one can cost under $15 at one pharmacy and much more at another. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I buy amoxicillin without a prescription?

No. In the United States, amoxicillin and other antibiotics for people require a prescription. Products sold online without a prescription, including fish or pet antibiotics, are not tested for human use. They may have the wrong dose or contain the wrong drug. Use a telehealth visit or a low-cost clinic instead.

Is the 875 mg tablet cheaper than two 500 mg capsules?

The doses are different, so the choice belongs to your prescriber. Price per dose can differ between strengths, though. If your prescriber says either strength works for you, compare the total price of each option.

Does insurance cover amoxicillin?

Almost every plan covers generic amoxicillin, usually in the lowest copay tier. Your copay can still be higher than the coupon price. Ask the pharmacist to check both prices.

What if I miss a dose?

Take the missed dose when you remember, unless the next dose is due soon. Do not take two doses at once. Ask your pharmacist for advice that fits your schedule.

How should I store amoxicillin?

Store capsules and tablets at room temperature in a dry place, away from the bathroom. Keep the liquid form as the label directs, and throw away any liquid left after the course ends.

The Bottom Line

Generic amoxicillin costs about $8 to $18 for a typical course without insurance. The most common way people overpay is to accept the first price at the counter. Compare prices first, ask for the coupon price, and use the antibiotic exactly as directed so one prescription does the job.
