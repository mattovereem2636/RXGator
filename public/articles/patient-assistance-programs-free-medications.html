Patient Assistance Programs: How to Get Free or Low-Cost Medications

Drug manufacturers, nonprofits, and government programs provide free medications to millions of Americans. Here's how to find and apply for them.

Millions of Americans qualify for free or deeply discounted medications through patient assistance programs — and most of them don't know it. Drug manufacturers, nonprofit organizations, and government agencies all run programs that provide medications at no cost or reduced cost to people who can't afford them.

Manufacturer Patient Assistance Programs (PAPs)

Nearly every major pharmaceutical company operates a patient assistance program for its brand-name drugs. These programs provide free medications to patients who are uninsured or underinsured and meet income eligibility requirements. The income limits vary by program but are often more generous than people expect — many programs cover patients with household incomes up to 300% or 400% of the federal poverty level.

To find manufacturer programs, search your medication on the drug company's website, look for a "patient assistance" or "savings" section. You can also use aggregator websites like NeedyMeds.org, RxAssist.org, or the Partnership for Prescription Assistance (pparx.org), which search across multiple manufacturer programs at once.

Applications typically require proof of income (tax returns or pay stubs), documentation of insurance status, and a prescription from your doctor. Processing takes two to six weeks in most cases. If approved, you'll receive a supply of medication — often 90 days — mailed directly or sent to your doctor's office. Programs are renewable, usually annually.

Copay Assistance and Savings Cards

For patients who have commercial insurance but face high copays on brand-name drugs, many manufacturers offer copay cards or savings programs. These cards cover part or all of your insurance copay, sometimes reducing it to $0. Unlike PAPs, copay cards are available regardless of income — the only major restriction is that they typically cannot be used by patients with government insurance (Medicare, Medicaid, Tricare).

Important for Medicare patients: Federal law prohibits manufacturer copay cards for government-insured patients. If you're on Medicare and struggling with drug costs, look into Extra Help (Low-Income Subsidy), State Pharmaceutical Assistance Programs (SPAPs), and Medicare-specific assistance from nonprofits like the Patient Access Network Foundation or the HealthWell Foundation.

Nonprofit and Foundation Programs

Several independent nonprofit foundations provide copay assistance for patients with specific diseases, regardless of which specific drug they take. The Patient Access Network Foundation (panfoundation.org), the HealthWell Foundation, and the Patient Advocate Foundation all offer grants to help cover out-of-pocket costs for patients with qualifying conditions. These programs often help Medicare patients who can't use manufacturer copay cards.

Government Programs

Medicaid provides prescription coverage for low-income individuals and families, with copays typically capped at a few dollars per prescription. The 340B Drug Pricing Program allows qualified healthcare organizations (including federally qualified health centers and certain hospitals) to purchase drugs at significantly reduced prices and pass those savings to patients. If you receive care at a community health center, you may already be benefiting from 340B pricing without knowing it.

State Pharmaceutical Assistance Programs (SPAPs) exist in many states and provide medication help to residents who don't qualify for Medicaid but can't afford prescriptions. Check your state health department's website for available programs.

You can also ask your doctor or pharmacist — they deal with these programs regularly and can often help with the application process. The key is asking. These programs exist specifically to help people who need them, and applying costs nothing.
