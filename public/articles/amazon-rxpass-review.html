Amazon RxPass Review: Is $5/Month Worth It?

All your eligible generic medications for one flat fee. We look at the drug list, the fine print, and who actually saves money.

Amazon RxPass is a $5 per month subscription that gives Prime members unlimited access to over 50 common generic medications. Not $5 per prescription — $5 total, for all your eligible meds combined, delivered free to your door. If you take multiple generics, the math can work out very well. But the drug list is limited, and the fine print matters.

How It Works

RxPass is an add-on to Amazon Prime ($139/year). For an additional $5 per month, you can get any combination of medications from the RxPass drug list filled as often as you need. Your doctor sends the prescription to Amazon Pharmacy, you set up auto-refill, and the medications arrive at your door in discreet packaging.

The $5 fee is charged on the first of each month regardless of whether you fill one medication or ten. If you don't fill anything that month, you're still charged. You can cancel anytime, and you keep access through the end of the paid month.

The value proposition is simple: If you currently pay more than $5 per month total for your generic medications, RxPass could save you money. If you take three generics that each cost $4 elsewhere, you're paying $12/month — RxPass cuts that to $5.

What's Covered

RxPass includes over 50 generic medications covering common conditions: high blood pressure (lisinopril, amlodipine, losartan), diabetes (metformin, glipizide), high cholesterol (atorvastatin, simvastatin, rosuvastatin), mental health (sertraline, escitalopram, bupropion, fluoxetine, citalopram, buspirone, trazodone), acid reflux (omeprazole, pantoprazole), thyroid (levothyroxine), allergies (montelukast, cetirizine), pain (meloxicam, naproxen), and several others.

Amazon says these medications treat over 80 common health conditions. The list is tilted toward high-volume maintenance medications — the drugs that tens of millions of Americans take daily.

What's NOT Covered

The list is deliberately limited. Brand-name drugs, controlled substances, specialty medications, insulin, inhalers, and many less common generics are not included. If your medication isn't on the RxPass list, Amazon Pharmacy still fills it — but at regular pricing, not the flat $5 rate.

RxPass is not available in California or Washington state, and people with Medicaid or CHIP coverage are not eligible. You cannot use HSA or FSA funds to pay the subscription fee.

The breakeven point is clear: if you take two or more medications on the RxPass list, it's almost certainly cheaper than any alternative. One medication makes it roughly equivalent to the Walmart $4 list. Zero medications means you're paying $5 for nothing.

Medicare and RxPass

As of 2024, Amazon expanded RxPass eligibility to Medicare beneficiaries. If you're on Medicare and take generics where your Part D copay exceeds the effective per-drug cost under RxPass, it's worth considering. Amazon estimates a Medicare beneficiary taking at least one RxPass medication could save approximately $70 per year. The savings increase with each additional medication on the list.

Important: RxPass is not insurance and is not a substitute for Medicare Part D. Purchases through RxPass don't count toward your Part D deductible or out-of-pocket cap.

The Bottom Line

RxPass is a strong deal for Prime members who take two or more common generics. It's less compelling for people who take just one medication (Walmart's $4 list is cheaper) or who take medications not on the RxPass list. The biggest limitation is the narrow drug list — Amazon has optimized for the highest-volume generics, which means many perfectly common medications are excluded.
