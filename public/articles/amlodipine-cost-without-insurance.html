How Much Does Amlodipine Cost Without Insurance in 2026?

Amlodipine is the #2 most prescribed drug in America and costs as little as $4 per month. Here's the full price breakdown and where to find the lowest cost.

Amlodipine besylate is a calcium channel blocker used to treat high blood pressure and angina (chest pain). It's the second most commonly prescribed medication in the United States, with over 80 million prescriptions filled annually. And it's one of the cheapest drugs you can take.

If you're paying out of pocket for amlodipine, you genuinely should not be spending more than $10 per month. In many cases, you can get it for $4. Here's the complete breakdown.

Brand-name Norvasc costs $300 to $450 per month. The patent expired in 2007. There is zero reason anyone should be paying for the brand version.

The math is simple: Amlodipine costs $4 at Walmart. If you're paying more than $10 anywhere else, you're overpaying. With a GoodRx coupon, you should be at $5 or less at most pharmacies.

Why Amlodipine Is So Cheap

Amlodipine is the poster child for how generic drugs should work. The patent expired almost 20 years ago. Multiple manufacturers produce it. The active ingredient is inexpensive to synthesize. Competition has driven the price to rock bottom.

This is what happens when the generic drug market works properly — and it's a sharp contrast to drugs like insulin, where patent games and market manipulation have kept prices artificially high for decades.

If you're taking amlodipine and struggling with the cost, something has gone wrong. Either you're at a pharmacy with excessive markup, or you're somehow still getting brand Norvasc. Either way, it's fixable.

How to Get the Lowest Price

1. Walmart's $4 List

Amlodipine in all three strengths (2.5mg, 5mg, and 10mg) is on Walmart's $4 prescription list. A 30-day supply is $4. A 90-day supply is $10. You don't need a coupon, a membership, or anything else. Just bring your prescription.

2. Costco Pharmacy

Costco consistently offers some of the lowest prescription prices in the country. You do not need a Costco membership to use their pharmacy — that's federal law. Amlodipine at Costco runs $3.50 to $5 for a 30-day supply.

3. Free Discount Cards

GoodRx and SingleCare both bring amlodipine to $5 or less at most pharmacies, including CVS and Walgreens where the cash price is $15+. These cards are free and take 30 seconds to use.

4. 90-Day Supply

For a drug you'll take every day, potentially for life, a 90-day fill saves both money and trips. Walmart charges $10 for a 90-day supply. Other pharmacies offer similar savings on longer fills.

Pro tip: If you're taking both amlodipine and another blood pressure medication, ask your doctor if a combination pill exists. Amlodipine/benazepril and amlodipine/valsartan combinations are available as generics and can simplify your regimen while potentially reducing your total cost.

Common Questions About Amlodipine Cost

Does the 10mg tablet cost more than the 5mg?

Usually not by much. At Walmart, all three strengths are $4. At other pharmacies, the 10mg may be $1 to $3 more per month. If your doctor prescribes 10mg and you want to save, you could ask about taking two 5mg tablets — but the savings are minimal with a drug this cheap.

Is there a generic for Norvasc?

Yes. Generic amlodipine besylate has been available since 2007. Every pharmacy carries it. If your prescription says "Norvasc," your pharmacist will automatically dispense the generic unless your doctor specifically wrote "dispense as written."

Can I get amlodipine through mail order?

Yes. Mail-order pharmacies like Amazon Pharmacy, Mark Cuban's Cost Plus Drugs, and others offer amlodipine at very low prices with home delivery. For a medication you take daily long-term, mail order can be convenient and affordable.

The Bottom Line

Amlodipine is one of the most affordable prescription drugs in America. At $4 per month at Walmart, it costs less than a cup of coffee. If you're paying significantly more than that, you have options: switch pharmacies, use a free discount card, or ask your pharmacist to check the lowest available price. There's no reason to overpay for a drug this common and this cheap.

High blood pressure is called the "silent killer" because most people have no symptoms. Taking your medication consistently is critical. Don't let cost be the reason you skip doses — because at these prices, cost shouldn't be a barrier.
