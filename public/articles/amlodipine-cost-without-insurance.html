How Much Does Amlodipine Cost Without Insurance in 2026?

Most people assume all blood pressure medications cost roughly the same. They don't. Amlodipine — the most prescribed calcium channel blocker in America — ranges from $4 at Walmart to $27 at a chain pharmacy two blocks away. And the price gap gets wider when you look across the entire class.

Amlodipine besylate is prescribed to roughly 75 million Americans each year for high blood pressure and chest pain. It is one of the most commonly dispensed medications in the country, yet its retail price varies by more than 500% depending on where and how you fill it. If you are paying without insurance, understanding that spread can save you hundreds of dollars over a year of treatment.

The Real Price Range for Amlodipine

For a standard 30-day supply of generic amlodipine besylate 5mg tablets, cash prices in 2026 fall into three broad tiers:

  • Low tier ($4–$6): Walmart's $4 generic list, pharmacy discount programs, and the best coupon prices through GoodRx or SingleCare.
  • Mid tier ($8–$15): Most independent pharmacies and some chain pharmacies with moderate markup over acquisition cost.
  • High tier ($20–$27+): Full retail cash price at chain pharmacies like CVS, Walgreens, or Rite Aid without any discount card or coupon applied.

The average retail price for amlodipine besylate 5mg, 30 tablets sits at approximately $27.19 without discounts. That figure comes from aggregated pharmacy pricing data and represents what you would pay if you walked in, handed over a prescription, and paid the sticker price.

Brand-name Norvasc, still available but rarely prescribed, runs $100 or more per month for the same 5mg strength. There is no clinical advantage to the brand version for the vast majority of patients.

Why prices vary so much for the same pill Pharmacies set their own retail cash prices. Two stores in the same zip code may buy amlodipine for pennies per tablet but mark it up differently based on their business model, foot traffic, and whether they use the pharmacy as a loss leader.

Calcium Channel Blocker Price Landscape

Amlodipine is the most affordable calcium channel blocker on the market, but it is not the only one prescribed for hypertension. If your doctor is considering alternatives within the same drug class, price differences are worth knowing.

Medication Typical Cash Price (30-day) Common Use

Nifedipine ER 30mg $10–$40 Hypertension, Raynaud's
Diltiazem ER 120mg $12–$55 Hypertension, rate control
Felodipine ER 5mg $15–$60 Hypertension

Amlodipine dominates on price for several reasons: it has the highest prescribing volume, which drives manufacturing competition; it was one of the earliest calcium channel blockers to lose patent protection; and its once-daily dosing makes it a first-line choice for physicians, keeping demand — and generic production — consistently high.

If your physician recommends switching to a different calcium channel blocker for clinical reasons, be aware that the price floor rises significantly. Diltiazem ER, in particular, has multiple extended-release formulations that are not interchangeable, which limits generic competition and keeps prices elevated.

The Norvasc Patent Story

Understanding why amlodipine is so cheap today requires a look at its history as one of the pharmaceutical industry's biggest revenue generators.

Pfizer launched Norvasc (brand-name amlodipine) in 1992. By the early 2000s, it had become the world's best-selling cardiovascular drug, with peak annual revenues reaching approximately $4.5 billion. At its height, a month's supply of Norvasc cost patients $120 or more.

The core patent expired in 2007. Generic manufacturers entered the market almost immediately, and the price trajectory followed the pattern seen with most blockbuster drugs: a steep initial drop followed by years of gradual decline as more manufacturers joined the market.

Today, more than a dozen generic manufacturers produce amlodipine besylate in the United States. That level of competition has driven the wholesale acquisition cost down to pennies per tablet. The branded version persists in pharmacies but accounts for a negligible share of prescriptions.

The Blockbuster-to-Commodity Pipeline

Amlodipine's pricing history is a textbook case of how the generic drug market works in America. A drug that generated billions in patent-protected revenue now costs less than a cup of coffee per month — if you know where to look. The challenge for consumers is that retail prices have not fallen as far as wholesale costs, creating the markup gap explored in the next section.

What Your Pharmacy Actually Pays (NADAC)

The National Average Drug Acquisition Cost (NADAC) is a survey-based benchmark published by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS). It reflects the actual price pharmacies pay wholesalers and manufacturers for each drug.

For generic amlodipine besylate 5mg, the NADAC hovers around $0.03 to $0.05 per tablet. That means a pharmacy's acquisition cost for a 30-day supply is roughly $0.90 to $1.50.

When a pharmacy charges $27 for that same bottle, the markup exceeds 1,700%. That margin is not pure profit — it covers the pharmacist's time, overhead, inventory carrying costs, and the reality that pharmacies lose money on many insurance-reimbursed prescriptions. But for cash-paying customers, the gap between what the pharmacy paid and what appears on the receipt is enormous.

Why this matters for uninsured patients Programs like Walmart's $4 list and Cost Plus Drugs price closer to actual acquisition cost. Cost Plus Drugs explicitly shows the formula: drug cost + 15% markup + a flat dispensing fee. That transparency is why their prices consistently undercut traditional pharmacies.

You can look up current NADAC data through the CMS Medicaid website. Comparing NADAC to quoted pharmacy prices gives you leverage when negotiating or choosing where to fill.

How to Get the Lowest Price

If you are paying out of pocket for amlodipine, these strategies will bring your cost to the floor:

  1. Start with Walmart or Costco. Both offer amlodipine near acquisition cost. Walmart's $4 list requires no signup. Costco requires no membership for pharmacy use.
  2. Check GoodRx and SingleCare. Free coupon services that negotiate contracted rates at participating pharmacies. Prices fluctuate, so check both before each fill.
  3. Consider Cost Plus Drugs. Mark Cuban's online pharmacy uses transparent, acquisition-plus-margin pricing. Orders ship by mail.
  4. Ask about 90-day supplies. Many pharmacies and discount programs offer deeper per-tablet discounts on 90-day fills, reducing trips and cost.
  5. Never pay the sticker price. The full retail cash price is the worst deal at any pharmacy. Even asking "do you have a discount card?" at the counter can cut the price significantly.
