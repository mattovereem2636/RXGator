How Much Does Gabapentin Cost Without Insurance in 2026?

Generic gabapentin costs $10 to $50 per month depending on dose and pharmacy. Here's where to find the best price and why brand Neurontin is never worth it.

Gabapentin is the fifth most prescribed drug in America. Originally approved for seizures, it's now used far more commonly for nerve pain, and doctors prescribe it off-label for everything from anxiety to restless leg syndrome. If you're paying out of pocket, gabapentin is generally affordable — but the price can swing wildly depending on your dose, your pharmacy, and whether you know to use a discount card.

Here's what gabapentin actually costs in 2026 and how to make sure you're paying the lowest price possible.

Gabapentin Price Breakdown by Dose

Gabapentin pricing depends heavily on the dose because higher doses mean more capsules per day. Most people take gabapentin three times daily, and doses range from 300mg to 800mg per dose. Here's what a 30-day supply costs at common doses:

Brand-name Neurontin costs $320 to $520 per month depending on the dose. The generic has been available since 2004. There is absolutely no clinical reason to take brand Neurontin, and no pharmacist would recommend it.

Best price: GoodRx typically brings gabapentin 300mg (90 capsules) down to about $11 at pharmacies like Costco, Walmart, and Kroger. That's the benchmark — if you're paying more, shop around.

Gabapentin Is a Controlled Substance in Some States

This is something that catches people off guard. Gabapentin is not a federally controlled substance, but several states — including Kentucky, West Virginia, Tennessee, Virginia, Michigan, and others — have classified it as a Schedule V controlled substance. More states have added it to their prescription drug monitoring programs.

What this means for you practically:

  • In controlled-substance states, you cannot transfer gabapentin prescriptions between pharmacies as easily.
  • Some pharmacies may require ID and may limit early refills.
  • Mail-order pharmacy options may be limited depending on your state.
  • Some discount programs have restrictions on controlled substances.
Check your state: Before switching pharmacies for a better price, verify that your state allows gabapentin prescription transfers. Your pharmacist can tell you in 30 seconds.

How to Save the Most on Gabapentin

1. Use a Free Discount Card

GoodRx, SingleCare, and RxSaver all offer free coupons for gabapentin. The difference between the cash price and the discount price at chain pharmacies can be $20 to $40 — that's real money over a year of fills.

2. Ask About 90-Day Fills

If you take gabapentin daily and your state allows it, a 90-day fill is almost always cheaper per pill. At Walmart, a 90-day supply of gabapentin 300mg is $10 versus $4 per month — saving you $2 over three months. At other pharmacies, the per-unit savings from 90-day fills can be much larger.

3. Consider Walmart First

Gabapentin is on Walmart's $4 prescription list. For many people, this is the cheapest option, no coupon or card needed. You just need a prescription from your doctor.

4. Talk to Your Doctor About Dose Consolidation

If you're taking three 300mg capsules three times daily (nine capsules total), your doctor might be able to prescribe the 800mg tablet plus a 100mg capsule instead, potentially reducing your pill count and sometimes your cost. Larger-strength tablets sometimes have a lower per-milligram price. Ask your pharmacist to check.

5. Avoid Brand Neurontin

This should go without saying, but if a prescription says "Neurontin" and the doctor hasn't written "dispense as written," your pharmacist will automatically substitute the generic. If for some reason your prescription specifies brand only, call your doctor and ask them to change it. You'll save $300+ per month.

What About Generic Gabapentin Alternatives?

If gabapentin isn't working well for you, the main alternative in the same drug class is pregabalin (brand name Lyrica). However, pregabalin is significantly more expensive — even the generic runs $30 to $90 per month, and it's a Schedule V controlled substance federally, not just in certain states. Unless gabapentin is ineffective for you, it's the better value.

For nerve pain specifically, other affordable options include certain antidepressants like duloxetine and amitriptyline, both available as cheap generics. Talk to your doctor about what makes sense for your situation.

The Bigger Picture

Gabapentin is a case study in how pharmacy pricing works in America. The same 90-capsule bottle can cost $4 at Walmart and $50 at CVS. The drug itself is dirt cheap to manufacture. The price difference is entirely markup. Knowing this, the single most valuable thing you can do is compare prices before you fill — every single time.
