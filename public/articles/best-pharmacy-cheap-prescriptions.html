The Best Pharmacies for Cheap Prescriptions

Not all pharmacies price the same. Here's how the major chains, warehouse clubs, and online options stack up for uninsured and cash-pay patients.

If you're paying cash for prescriptions — whether you're uninsured, underinsured, or just paying less than your insurance copay — where you fill matters enormously. The same generic drug can vary by 500% or more between pharmacies in the same town. Here's how the major options stack up.

Walmart and Sam's Club

Walmart's generic drug discount program covers roughly 300 medications at flat-rate pricing — typically $4 for 30 days or $10 for 90 days. No membership, no coupon, no insurance needed. For the drugs on their list, it's hard to beat. Sam's Club pharmacies honor the same pricing. The limitation: only generics at commonly prescribed doses, and the list can change without notice.

Costco

Costco's pharmacy prices on generics are consistently among the lowest in the country — often lower than Walmart's flat rate on drugs not covered by Walmart's program. And by law in most states, you don't need a Costco membership to use the pharmacy. The downside is limited locations and shorter hours compared to chain pharmacies.

Cost Plus Drugs

Mark Cuban's mail-order pharmacy sells generics at the actual manufacturer cost plus a flat 15% markup and a small shipping fee. For maintenance medications where you can plan ahead and wait for delivery, Cost Plus is often the absolute cheapest option — especially for less common generics where other pharmacies charge high markups. They carry over 2,000 medications. The limitation: mail-order only, no same-day fills, no controlled substances, no brand-name drugs.

Amazon Pharmacy / RxPass

Amazon Pharmacy offers competitive pricing for Prime members, and its RxPass subscription covers 50+ common generics for a flat monthly fee. If you take multiple medications on the RxPass list, the per-drug cost is very low. The limitation: requires Prime membership, limited drug list for the flat-fee program, not available in all states.

Chain Pharmacies (CVS, Walgreens, Rite Aid)

Chain pharmacies generally have the highest cash prices on generics. However, with a free discount card from GoodRx or SingleCare, prices drop significantly — often matching or approaching warehouse club pricing. Chains have the advantage of convenience: more locations, longer hours, and drive-through windows. If you're going to use a chain, always use a discount card.

Grocery Store Pharmacies

Kroger, H-E-B, Meijer, and other grocery chains often have competitive generic pricing, sometimes with their own discount programs. Meijer has historically offered select generics for free. Grocery store pharmacies fly under the radar but can be excellent options, especially in areas without a Costco or Walmart nearby.

Independent Pharmacies

Independent pharmacies can go either way. Some offer personalized service and competitive pricing. Others have significantly higher cash prices due to lower volume and higher acquisition costs. Always check the price before filling at an independent — don't assume the convenience of a neighborhood pharmacy comes at a reasonable cost.

The bottom line: No single pharmacy is cheapest for everything. Walmart wins on its $4 list, Costco wins on many other generics, Cost Plus wins on less common medications, and Amazon wins if you take multiple RxPass-eligible drugs. The smartest strategy is to compare prices for your specific medications using RxGator, then fill wherever the best deal is — even if that means using different pharmacies for different drugs.
