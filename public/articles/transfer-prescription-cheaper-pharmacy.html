How to Transfer Your Prescription to a Cheaper Pharmacy

Switching pharmacies is easier than most people think and can save you hundreds per year. Here's the step-by-step process.

You found out your medication costs $35 at your current pharmacy and $6 at the one down the street. Can you just switch? Yes. Transferring a prescription is routine, usually takes a single phone call, and your new pharmacy handles most of the work.

How the Transfer Works

You have two options. The easiest: call or visit the new pharmacy and ask them to transfer your prescription. Give them your name, date of birth, the medication name and dose, and the name and phone number of your current pharmacy. The new pharmacist will call your old pharmacy, verify the prescription details, and transfer it. You don't need to contact your old pharmacy yourself.

Alternatively, you can ask your doctor to send a new prescription directly to the new pharmacy. This is sometimes necessary for controlled substances, which have restrictions on transfers in some states.

How long does it take? Most transfers are completed within a few hours. Some pharmacies can do it while you wait. If the old pharmacy is closed or busy, it might take until the next business day. Plan ahead so you don't run out of medication during the switch.

What You Need to Know

There's no fee to transfer a prescription. Pharmacies do it regularly and it's a normal part of the business. You don't owe your current pharmacy any loyalty or explanation — just like you can switch grocery stores, you can switch pharmacies anytime.

Most prescriptions can be transferred, but there are exceptions. Controlled substances (Schedule II drugs like certain opioids and stimulants) cannot be transferred in many states and may require a new prescription from your doctor. Prescriptions with no refills remaining can't be transferred — you'll need a new prescription. And some states limit the number of times a prescription can be transferred.

Your prescription history stays accessible. Pharmacies maintain records, and your new pharmacy can see your fill history through shared databases. Your insurance will work the same way at any in-network pharmacy.

When to Transfer

Compare prices before every new prescription and at least once a year for ongoing medications. Drug prices change, pharmacy discount programs change, and new options appear regularly. Use RxGator to compare prices across pharmacies and discount programs — then transfer to wherever the best deal is. There's no rule that says you have to fill all your prescriptions at the same place, either. You can use Walmart for one drug and Costco for another if the prices work out better that way.

The most common reason people overpay for prescriptions isn't that cheap options don't exist — it's that they stay at the same pharmacy out of habit without checking whether there's a better price available. A five-minute price comparison and a single phone call can save real money every month.
