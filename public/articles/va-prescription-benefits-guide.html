VA Prescription Benefits: What Veterans Should Know About Drug Costs

The VA pharmacy benefit is one of the best deals in American healthcare — if you know how to use it. Here is what veterans need to know about eligibility, copays, and getting the lowest prices on medications.

How VA Pharmacy Benefits Work

The Department of Veterans Affairs operates one of the largest pharmacy systems in the United States, serving millions of veterans through VA medical centers, community-based outpatient clinics, and a nationwide mail-order program. For eligible veterans, the VA pharmacy benefit offers prescription medications at copays that are a fraction of what most Americans pay at retail pharmacies.

The VA uses a straightforward two-tier copay structure. For a 30-day supply of medication, veterans in Priority Groups 2 through 6 pay $5 for preferred generic medications and $11 for brand-name or non-preferred drugs. These copays are among the lowest in the country — consider that the average retail copay for a brand-name drug through commercial insurance is over $50, and many Medicare Part D plans charge $40 or more for Tier 3 medications. The VA's pricing is remarkably competitive.

Veterans in Priority Group 1 — those with service-connected disabilities rated at 50% or higher — pay no copays at all for their prescriptions. Veterans with service-connected conditions also pay nothing for medications that treat those specific conditions, regardless of their Priority Group. Additionally, the VA caps annual outpatient copay costs, providing a financial ceiling that protects veterans who take multiple medications.

VA Mail-Order Pharmacy: Convenience and Savings

The VA's Consolidated Mail Outpatient Pharmacy (CMOP) system is one of its greatest strengths. The VA operates several large automated mail-order pharmacies across the country that can fill and ship prescriptions directly to a veteran's home, usually within three to five business days with free shipping. For maintenance medications — drugs you take regularly for ongoing conditions — mail order is often the most convenient option.

Key point: VA mail-order prescriptions are typically filled for a 90-day supply at the same copay as a 30-day supply from a VA outpatient pharmacy. That means a 90-day supply of a preferred generic costs $5 through CMOP, compared to $15 for three separate 30-day fills. For veterans on multiple medications, this adds up to significant savings over the course of a year.

Veterans can request mail-order refills through My HealtheVet (the VA's online patient portal), the VA Health and Benefits mobile app, by phone through the automated refill line, or by mailing in a refill slip. The system is designed to be as easy as possible, and prescriptions can be set up for automatic refills so veterans do not have to remember to reorder each time.

How the VA Gets Such Low Drug Prices

The VA pays less for medications than virtually any other purchaser in the U.S. healthcare system. This is not an accident — it is the result of federal law. Under the Veterans Health Care Act of 1992, pharmaceutical manufacturers must sell drugs to the VA at prices no higher than 76% of the non-federal average manufacturer price, known as the Federal Ceiling Price. In practice, the VA often negotiates prices well below this ceiling through its national formulary process.

The VA's Pharmacy Benefits Management (PBM) office maintains the VA National Formulary, a list of preferred drugs that VA pharmacies stock and prescribe. When a therapeutic class has multiple competing drugs, the VA's formulary committee evaluates clinical evidence and negotiates with manufacturers, often securing substantial discounts by agreeing to place a drug on the formulary in exchange for a lower price. This leverage — the ability to direct millions of patients toward one product over its competitors — gives the VA negotiating power that few other organizations can match.

The result is that VA drug prices are typically 40% to 60% lower than prices available through Medicare Part D or commercial insurance. For some expensive specialty medications, the difference can be even more dramatic. This pricing advantage is one reason why veterans who are eligible for VA care should strongly consider using VA pharmacy services, even if they also carry private insurance or Medicare.

Eligibility and Getting Started

To use VA pharmacy benefits, a veteran must first be enrolled in VA health care. Eligibility for VA health care is based on factors including the veteran's discharge status (generally, an honorable or other than dishonorable discharge is required), length of service, service-connected disabilities, income level, and other criteria. Veterans are assigned to one of eight Priority Groups based on these factors, which determines their copay obligations and access to care.

Watch out: Many veterans assume they are not eligible for VA health care because they do not have a service-connected disability. In reality, the VA serves veterans across a wide range of eligibility criteria, including combat veterans from recent conflicts (who receive five years of enhanced eligibility), veterans with limited income, and others. It is worth applying even if you are not sure you qualify — the enrollment process is free, and you may be surprised.

To enroll, veterans can apply online at va.gov/health-care/apply, call the VA Health Benefits Hotline at 1-877-222-8387, visit a local VA medical center's enrollment office, or mail in a completed VA Form 10-10EZ. Once enrolled, a veteran can begin receiving prescriptions from VA providers and filling them at VA pharmacies or through mail order.

VA Pricing vs. Retail: When to Use Which

For most medications, the VA pharmacy will be the cheapest option available to an eligible veteran. However, there are situations where comparing VA prices with retail alternatives makes sense. If a veteran needs a medication urgently and cannot wait for a VA appointment or mail-order delivery, filling at a retail pharmacy may be necessary. In those cases, using a discount tool like RxGator to compare prices across retail pharmacies and discount programs can help minimize the cost of filling outside the VA system.

Some veterans also carry Medicare Part D or private insurance alongside their VA benefits. In these cases, it pays to compare the cost of filling a specific medication through the VA versus through their other coverage. For inexpensive generics, the difference may be small. For brand-name or specialty drugs, the VA's Federal Supply Schedule pricing almost always wins. RxGator includes VA Federal Supply Schedule benchmark pricing in its comparisons, giving veterans a reference point to see how VA prices stack up against retail and discount options.

The bottom line for veterans is simple: if you are eligible for VA health care and you are not using VA pharmacy services, you are very likely paying more than you need to for your medications. The combination of low copays, free mail-order shipping, 90-day supplies, and the VA's negotiated drug prices makes this one of the most valuable benefits available to those who have served.
