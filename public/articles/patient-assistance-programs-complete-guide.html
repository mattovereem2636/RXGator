Patient Assistance Programs: The Complete Guide to Free Medications

Pharmaceutical companies give away billions of dollars in free medication every year. Here is how to find out if you qualify — and exactly how to apply.

What Are Patient Assistance Programs?

Patient Assistance Programs (PAPs) are programs run by pharmaceutical manufacturers that provide brand-name prescription medications at no cost to patients who cannot afford them. Nearly every major drug company in the United States operates one, and collectively these programs deliver free medication to millions of Americans each year.

If you are uninsured, underinsured, or simply cannot afford a medication your doctor prescribed, a PAP may be the single most powerful tool available to you. Unlike discount cards or coupons that reduce a price, PAPs eliminate the cost entirely — you pay nothing for your medication.

Here is what makes PAPs different from other savings options:

  • The medication is free. Not discounted, not reduced — free. You receive the actual brand-name drug at zero cost.
  • They cover expensive medications. PAPs are especially valuable for specialty drugs, biologics, and brand-name medications that can cost hundreds or thousands of dollars per month.
  • They ship directly to you or your doctor. Most programs mail medication to your prescriber's office or, in some cases, directly to your home.
  • Enrollment typically lasts 12 months and can be renewed annually as long as you continue to meet eligibility requirements.

Who Qualifies for Patient Assistance Programs?

Eligibility varies by program, but most PAPs share a common set of requirements:

  • U.S. residency. You must be a legal resident of the United States.
  • No insurance coverage for the medication. Most programs require that you have no prescription drug insurance, or that your insurance does not cover the specific medication. Some programs also accept patients whose insurance leaves them with high out-of-pocket costs.
  • Income below a certain threshold. This is typically expressed as a percentage of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL). Most programs set their cutoff between 200% and 600% of FPL.
  • A valid prescription. You need a prescriber (doctor, nurse practitioner, or physician assistant) who is willing to sign the application.

2026 Federal Poverty Level Income Reference

The 2026 FPL for a single individual in the contiguous 48 states is $15,960. Here is what common PAP thresholds look like in actual dollar amounts for a single person:

How to Apply: Step by Step

The application process is similar across most PAPs. Here is what to expect:

Step 1: Identify the Right Program

Find out which company manufactures your medication, then visit their PAP website (see the table above). If you are not sure which company makes your drug, use Medicine Assistance Tool (medicineassistancetool.org) or NeedyMeds.org to search by drug name. Both are free databases that match medications to available assistance programs.

Step 2: Check Eligibility

Most program websites include a quick eligibility screener. You will typically need to know your household size, annual income, and insurance status. This takes about two minutes and tells you immediately whether you are likely to qualify.

Step 3: Download and Complete the Application

PAP applications are usually 2–4 pages and require:

  • Your personal information (name, address, date of birth)
  • Income documentation (a recent tax return, pay stubs, or a signed income attestation form)
  • Insurance information (or a statement that you are uninsured)
  • Your prescriber's signature and DEA/NPI number
  • The prescription itself (drug name, dosage, and quantity)

Step 4: Have Your Doctor Sign and Submit

Your prescriber must sign the application. Many doctor's offices have staff who handle PAP paperwork regularly — ask the office manager or a medical assistant for help. Some programs now accept electronic applications submitted directly by the prescriber's office.

Step 5: Wait for Approval

Processing typically takes 2–4 weeks. Some programs offer expedited processing for urgent cases. During this waiting period, ask your doctor about samples or short-term alternatives to bridge the gap.

Step 6: Receive Your Medication

Once approved, medication is shipped — usually to your doctor's office, where you pick it up. Some programs ship directly to your home or to a designated pharmacy. You will typically receive a 90-day supply and can reorder as needed throughout your enrollment year.

Step 7: Renew Annually

Most PAPs require annual renewal. The program will notify you before your enrollment expires. Renewal is usually simpler than the initial application.

How to Search for PAPs: Free Tools

If you take multiple medications or are not sure where to start, these free search tools can help you find every program you might qualify for:

  • NeedyMeds.org — The most comprehensive database of patient assistance programs, discount drug cards, and disease-specific resources. Search by drug name or browse by condition.
  • Medicine Assistance Tool (medicineassistancetool.org) — Operated by PhRMA, this tool matches your medications to manufacturer PAPs and walks you through the application process.

What If No PAP Exists for Your Medication?

Not every drug is covered by a PAP. Generic medications, for example, rarely have manufacturer assistance programs because they are already lower-cost. If you cannot find a PAP for your medication, here are alternatives worth exploring:

Prescription Discount Cards and Coupons

Free discount cards from services like GoodRx, RxSaver, and RxGator can reduce the cost of generics and some brand-name drugs significantly. Search RxGator to compare prices across 18+ sources for your specific medication and dosage.

Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHCs)

FQHCs are community health centers that receive federal funding to serve underserved populations. They provide medical care on a sliding fee scale based on income, and many have on-site pharmacies that offer medications at deeply reduced 340B pricing. Find one near you at findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov.

Nonprofit and Mail-Order Pharmacies

  • Rx Outreach (rxoutreach.org) — A nonprofit mail-order pharmacy offering over 1,000 generic medications at reduced prices for qualifying patients.
  • SIRUM / Good Pill (goodpill.org) — Redistributes donated, unexpired medications to patients in need. Many medications are available for a few dollars per month.

Disease-Specific Foundations and Copay Assistance

Several independent nonprofit foundations provide grants to help patients cover medication costs for specific diseases:

  • PAN Foundation (Patient Access Network Foundation) — Provides copay assistance for patients with specific chronic and rare diseases. Unlike manufacturer PAPs, PAN can help patients who have insurance but face high copays.
  • HealthWell Foundation — Offers copay and premium assistance for underinsured patients across dozens of disease categories.
  • NORD (National Organization for Rare Disorders) — Operates its own patient assistance programs and maintains a directory of resources for patients with rare diseases.

Tips for a Successful Application

  • Apply to every program you might qualify for. If you take multiple brand-name medications, submit separate applications to each manufacturer's PAP.
  • Ask your doctor's office for help. Many practices have a staff member who handles PAP paperwork. Social workers at hospitals and clinics are also excellent resources.
  • Do not wait until you run out of medication. Processing takes 2–4 weeks. Apply as soon as you know you need help affording a drug.
  • Keep copies of everything. Make copies of your completed application and all supporting documents before submitting.
  • Follow up. If you have not heard back within 4 weeks, call the program's phone number to check your application status.
  • Be honest about your finances. Programs verify income, and providing inaccurate information can result in denial or removal from the program.

The Bottom Line

Patient Assistance Programs are one of the most underused resources in American healthcare. If you are struggling to afford a brand-name medication, there is a real chance that the company that makes it will provide it to you for free. The application process takes some effort, but the payoff — a year of free medication — is worth it.

Start by searching for your medication on NeedyMeds.org or the Medicine Assistance Tool, then talk to your doctor about signing the application. And for medications where a PAP is not available, search RxGator to find the lowest price across pharmacies, discount cards, and other savings programs.

You should not have to choose between paying for medication and paying for everything else. These programs exist to make sure you do not have to.
