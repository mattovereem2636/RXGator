How Much Does Losartan Cost Without Insurance in 2026?

Generic losartan costs $4 to $30 per month. It's one of the most affordable blood pressure medications available. Here's how to find the lowest price.

Losartan potassium is the sixth most prescribed drug in the United States. It belongs to a class of medications called ARBs (angiotensin II receptor blockers) and is primarily used to treat high blood pressure. For many patients, losartan is the go-to alternative when ACE inhibitors like lisinopril cause a persistent dry cough — a common side effect that affects up to 15% of ACE inhibitor users.

The good news: generic losartan is cheap. Really cheap. Here's exactly what you'll pay and how to make sure you're getting the best deal.

Losartan vs. Lisinopril: Why People Switch

Losartan and lisinopril are both first-line blood pressure medications, and they're similarly effective. The main reason people end up on losartan instead of lisinopril is the ACE inhibitor cough.

ACE inhibitors (lisinopril, enalapril, benazepril) cause a dry, persistent cough in about 10-15% of patients. It's not dangerous, but it's annoying enough that many people switch to an ARB like losartan, which doesn't cause this side effect.

From a cost perspective, the two drugs are nearly identical:

Both are on Walmart's $4 list. Both are widely available as generics. If your doctor recommends switching from lisinopril to losartan because of side effects, you won't pay more. Cost should never be the barrier to making this switch.

Don't just stop lisinopril: If you're getting the ACE inhibitor cough, do not stop taking your blood pressure medication on your own. Call your doctor and ask to switch to losartan or another ARB. Stopping blood pressure medication without a replacement can be dangerous.

How to Get the Lowest Price on Losartan

1. Start at Walmart

Losartan 25mg, 50mg, and 100mg are all on Walmart's $4 prescription list. A 30-day supply is $4, and a 90-day supply is $10. This is consistently the cheapest option for most people. No coupon, no membership, no card. Just a prescription.

2. Use GoodRx or SingleCare

If Walmart isn't convenient, a free discount card from GoodRx or SingleCare brings losartan down to $5 to $9 at most pharmacies. The savings are biggest at chain pharmacies like CVS and Walgreens, where the cash price is $20+.

3. Get a 90-Day Supply

Blood pressure medication is something you take every single day, indefinitely. A 90-day fill saves money and reduces pharmacy trips. At Walmart, a 90-day supply of losartan is $10. At other pharmacies with a discount card, 90-day fills typically save 15-25% compared to three separate 30-day fills.

4. Consider Costco

Costco's pharmacy prices are consistently among the lowest. You don't need a Costco membership to use their pharmacy. Losartan 50mg runs about $4 to $7 for a 30-day supply at Costco.

5. Check Mail-Order Options

For a long-term maintenance medication like losartan, mail-order pharmacies can be both convenient and cheap. Cost Plus Drugs (Mark Cuban's pharmacy) and Amazon Pharmacy both offer losartan at competitive prices with home delivery.

What About Losartan Combination Pills?

Losartan is also available combined with hydrochlorothiazide (a diuretic) in a single pill called losartan/HCTZ. This combination is commonly prescribed when losartan alone isn't enough to control blood pressure.

Generic losartan/HCTZ (50mg/12.5mg) costs slightly more than losartan alone — typically $8 to $20 per month, or $5 to $12 with a discount card. It's still very affordable, and taking one pill instead of two improves adherence, which is ultimately what matters for blood pressure control.

Why This Matters

Nearly half of American adults have high blood pressure, and only about one in four have it under control. One of the top reasons people don't take their blood pressure medication is cost. But with losartan at $4 per month, cost genuinely is not the issue — awareness is.

If you or someone you know is skipping blood pressure medication because of the expense, the fix might be as simple as switching to losartan and filling it at Walmart. That's $48 per year. Less than a dollar a week. For a medication that significantly reduces your risk of heart attack, stroke, and kidney disease.
