How Much Does Losartan Cost Without Insurance in 2026?

Losartan is one of the most widely prescribed blood pressure medications in the country, and as a mature generic, it can be remarkably affordable — if you know where to look. Here is what it actually costs, and how to avoid overpaying.

What Losartan Actually Costs at the Pharmacy

Losartan is an angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB) used to treat high blood pressure, protect kidney function in people with type 2 diabetes, and reduce the risk of stroke. It has been available as a generic since 2010 when the patent on the brand-name version, Cozaar, expired. Generic competition has driven the wholesale cost down considerably, but retail pharmacy prices still vary more than most people realize.

If you walk into a pharmacy without insurance and ask for a 30-day supply of generic losartan potassium, you could pay anywhere from $4 to $55 depending on the pharmacy, the dosage, and whether you use a discount program. The most commonly prescribed dosages are 25 mg, 50 mg, and 100 mg. Pricing tends to be similar across dosages, though the 100 mg strength can sometimes run a few dollars more.

With a free discount card from GoodRx, SingleCare, or RxSaver, prices at major chain pharmacies typically fall to $4 to $10 for a 30-day supply. Walmart includes losartan on its discount generic program at $9 for a 30-day supply and $24 for 90 days. Costco pharmacy, accessible without a membership in most states, offers similarly competitive pricing.

Without a discount card, the retail cash price at CVS, Walgreens, or Rite Aid can land in the $35 to $55 range. That spread — from $4 with a coupon to $55 without — is one of the widest you will see for any common generic, and it is exactly why price comparison matters.

Key point: The retail cash price for losartan can be 10 times higher than the discount card price at the same pharmacy. A free coupon can save you $40 or more per month.

Generic Losartan vs. Brand-Name Cozaar

Losartan was originally marketed as Cozaar by Merck. The brand version is still available, but there is no clinical reason to choose it over the generic. Generic losartan potassium contains the identical active ingredient in the same dosage and is held to the same FDA manufacturing standards for purity, potency, and bioavailability.

The price difference tells the whole story. Brand-name Cozaar can cost $140 to $200 or more for a 30-day supply without insurance, while generic losartan with a discount card runs $4 to $10. Unless your prescription specifically says "dispense as written," your pharmacist will automatically fill the generic. If you see an unexpected price at the counter, confirm you are receiving the generic version.

Losartan Combination Pills: Losartan/HCTZ

Many patients take losartan in combination with hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ), a diuretic, in a single pill. This combination — sold generically as losartan/HCTZ and formerly branded as Hyzaar — is also available as an affordable generic. Prices for the combination tablet are slightly higher than losartan alone, typically $8 to $20 for a 30-day supply with a discount card, compared to $30 to $70 at full retail price.

If your doctor has prescribed the combination, it is worth comparing prices separately. In some cases, filling losartan and HCTZ as two individual generics can be cheaper than the combination pill, especially at pharmacies with $4 generic lists. Ask your pharmacist to check both options.

Where to Find the Lowest Price

Free discount card programs are typically the cheapest route for losartan. GoodRx, SingleCare, and RxSaver regularly show prices of $4 to $8 at major chains including CVS, Walgreens, Kroger, and Walmart. No enrollment or insurance is needed — the cards work like an instant coupon.

Retailer pharmacy programs are a reliable fallback. Walmart and Sam's Club list losartan at $9 for 30 days and $24 for 90 days, with no card or app required. Several grocery store chains — Kroger, Meijer, and Publix among them — offer similar pricing on their discount generic lists.

Cost Plus Drugs, the Mark Cuban venture, sells losartan at close to wholesale cost plus a transparent markup and flat dispensing fee, usually landing around $4 to $6 for a 30-day supply shipped to your door. For patients in areas with limited pharmacy competition, mail-order options like this can be a real money saver.

If you take losartan daily, a 90-day supply is almost always the better value. Walmart charges $24 for 90 days, and discount cards often show 90-tablet prices of $10 to $18 — less than $6 per month.

Watch out: Losartan/HCTZ combination tablets are priced separately from plain losartan. If your doctor prescribes the combo, check whether two separate generics would cost less.

Insurance Copays vs. Cash Price

As with many inexpensive generics, your insurance copay for losartan might be higher than what you would pay out of pocket with a discount card. Tier 1 generic copays of $5 to $15 are common, but if a discount card gets you losartan for $4, cash is the better deal.

The tradeoff is that cash payments do not count toward your annual deductible or out-of-pocket maximum, while insurance copays do. For a medication this cheap, the difference is unlikely to matter unless you are close to hitting a threshold and have other expensive prescriptions. But it is worth being aware of, especially during open enrollment season when you are evaluating plan costs.
