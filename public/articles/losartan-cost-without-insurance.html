How Much Does Losartan Cost Without Insurance in 2026?

A single losartan tablet costs your pharmacy about three cents. By the time it reaches you at the counter, the price has been multiplied anywhere from 15x to 150x. For a standard 30-day supply of losartan potassium 50mg—the most commonly prescribed strength—you could pay as little as $4.98 or as much as $45.99 at full retail, depending entirely on where you fill the prescription and whether you use a discount tool.

That gap between $5 and $46 for the same 30 tablets of the same molecule is not a typo. It is the direct result of a supply chain where every intermediary adds margin on top of a rock-bottom manufacturing cost. Understanding where those dollars go is the first step toward not overpaying.

The Supply Chain Markup: From $0.03 to $46

The National Average Drug Acquisition Cost (NADAC), published weekly by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, tracks what pharmacies actually pay their wholesalers for each generic drug. For losartan potassium 50mg tablets, the NADAC hovers around $0.03 per tablet, or roughly $0.90 for a 30-count bottle. That figure reflects the pharmacy's wholesale purchase price after rebates.

Here is how that multiplication happens at each stage:

  • Manufacturer to wholesaler: Bulk generic losartan is produced by multiple manufacturers (Teva, Aurobindo, Lupin, and others), keeping raw ingredient costs minimal. Active pharmaceutical ingredient costs for losartan are well under a penny per tablet. The manufacturer sells to wholesalers like McKesson, AmerisourceBergen, or Cardinal Health at around $0.02–$0.03 per tablet.
  • Wholesaler to pharmacy: The three major wholesalers distribute to retail pharmacies, typically adding a 2–5% margin. At $0.03 NADAC, the wholesaler markup is nearly invisible in dollar terms but significant in percentage terms on such a cheap base product.
  • Pharmacy to patient: This is where the largest gap opens. Pharmacies set their own cash prices, and the usual and customary (U&C) price for losartan 50mg at a major chain pharmacy runs $35–$46 for 30 tablets. That means the pharmacy’s gross margin on an uninsured cash-pay prescription can exceed 4,000%. Dispensing fees, overhead, labor, and the pharmacy benefit model all contribute to this markup, but the sheer scale surprises most patients.
Key Takeaway

The drug itself is almost free. What you are paying for at full retail is pharmacy overhead, PBM contracts, and a pricing system designed around insurance reimbursement—not around the actual cost of the medication.

Discount Programs and Coupon Cards

If you do not have insurance, or if your insurance copay for losartan exceeds the discount price, several programs can bring the cost down substantially:

  • Walmart $4 Generics Program: Losartan is on Walmart's $4 list for a 30-day supply and $10 for a 90-day supply. This is often the absolute lowest price available at a brick-and-mortar pharmacy. No coupon or card required—just ask for the program price.
  • Costco Pharmacy: Costco pharmacies are open to non-members by law in most states. Their cash prices on generics are consistently among the lowest of any retail pharmacy, typically $5.40–$7.20 for losartan 50mg, 30 tablets.
  • Mark Cuban Cost Plus Drugs: Cost Plus Drugs uses a transparent pricing model: manufacturer acquisition cost plus a 15% markup plus a flat pharmacy dispensing fee. For losartan, this typically comes to around $5.10 for 30 tablets. Orders are filled by mail.
  • GoodRx: A free coupon aggregator that pulls discount prices from multiple pharmacy benefit managers. Current GoodRx prices for losartan 50mg, 30 tablets, start around $7.30 depending on your ZIP code and selected pharmacy.
  • SingleCare: Similar to GoodRx, SingleCare negotiates discount rates with pharmacies. Prices typically range from $7.50 to $10.00 for losartan at major chains. The card is free and accepted at over 35,000 pharmacies.
Compare Before You Fill

Discount card prices change frequently and vary by pharmacy location. Always check at least two sources before filling a prescription. A five-minute price check can save $30 or more on a single fill.

ARB Class Price Comparison

Losartan belongs to the angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB) drug class. All ARBs work by blocking the same receptor, but their prices vary significantly. If you are paying out of pocket, the cost difference between ARBs can be substantial over a year of treatment.

Losartan is typically the cheapest ARB by a significant margin. This price advantage is partly due to the number of generic manufacturers producing it—losartan was the first ARB to lose patent protection (in 2010), giving generic competition a substantial head start over newer ARBs. For patients without insurance, the annual savings of choosing losartan over candesartan could exceed $400.

Why Losartan Replaced Lisinopril for Many Patients

Before considering an ARB like losartan, most prescribers start patients on an ACE inhibitor such as lisinopril. ACE inhibitors are first-line therapy for hypertension and are even cheaper than ARBs—generic lisinopril 10mg costs as little as $3.00 for a 30-day supply.

So why do roughly 10% of patients on ACE inhibitors end up switching to losartan? The answer is a persistent, dry cough caused by ACE inhibitors. This side effect, driven by bradykinin accumulation, affects an estimated 5–15% of ACE inhibitor users and is more common in women and patients of East Asian descent. The cough is not dangerous but is often disruptive enough that patients cannot tolerate the medication long-term.

ARBs like losartan block the angiotensin II receptor directly without affecting bradykinin metabolism, which means the cough side effect essentially disappears. For the subset of patients who cannot tolerate ACE inhibitors, losartan is the logical next step because it provides similar blood pressure reduction at the lowest cost in the ARB class.

Cost Impact of the Switch

Switching from lisinopril to losartan does increase out-of-pocket cost. At the lowest available prices, the difference is modest—roughly $2 per month ($3 for lisinopril vs. $5 for losartan). At full retail without discounts, the gap widens to $10–$15 per month. For uninsured patients, using a discount program narrows this gap to near zero.

Brand Cozaar vs. Generic Losartan

Losartan was originally marketed as Cozaar by Merck. The brand-name version is still available but costs dramatically more—often $200 or more for a 30-day supply at retail. There is no clinical difference between brand Cozaar and generic losartan potassium; they contain the same active ingredient at the same dose and must meet the same FDA bioequivalence standards. Unless your prescriber has a specific, documented reason for requiring the brand, generic losartan is the appropriate choice, particularly without insurance.

How to Get the Lowest Price

Based on current 2026 pricing, here are the most reliable strategies for minimizing your out-of-pocket cost for losartan without insurance:

  1. Ask for a 90-day supply. Many discount programs and pharmacies offer significantly lower per-tablet pricing for 90-day fills. Walmart charges $10 for 90 tablets vs. $4.98 for 30, saving you roughly $5 per quarter.
  2. Compare at least three sources. Check Walmart, Costco, and one discount card (GoodRx or SingleCare) before every fill. Prices shift month to month.
  3. Consider mail-order. Cost Plus Drugs and other mail-order pharmacies eliminate retail overhead and often pass those savings through to patients.
  4. Ask your pharmacist about the NADAC. Pharmacists can see their acquisition cost. While they are not obligated to price-match, knowing the baseline gives you leverage in any pricing conversation.
  5. Check state pharmaceutical assistance programs. Many states offer drug assistance programs for uninsured residents that cover common generics including losartan.

The markup on generic medications like losartan is invisible unless you compare.
