How Much Does Omeprazole Cost Without Insurance in 2026?

Generic omeprazole runs $5 to $25 per month — and it's available over the counter. Here's how to find the lowest price and whether OTC is cheaper than prescription.

Omeprazole is the ninth most prescribed drug in America. Millions of people take it every day for acid reflux, GERD, heartburn, and stomach ulcers. It belongs to a class of drugs called proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) that reduce the amount of acid your stomach produces.

The good news is that omeprazole is genuinely cheap, even without insurance. The less obvious news is that you have two separate paths to buying it — prescription and over the counter — and the better choice depends on your situation. Let's break down exactly what you'll pay and where to find the lowest price.

Bottom line: With a GoodRx or SingleCare coupon, you should be paying $5 to $9 per month for prescription omeprazole at most pharmacies. At Walmart, it's $4 flat — no coupon needed.

OTC vs. Prescription Omeprazole: Which Is Cheaper?

Here's where it gets interesting. Omeprazole is one of the few drugs available both by prescription and over the counter. Prilosec OTC contains the same 20mg dose as the most common prescription strength. But the pricing works differently.

Prilosec OTC (or store-brand OTC omeprazole) typically costs $15 to $25 for a 42-count box, which is a 42-day supply if you take one pill per day. That works out to roughly $11 to $18 per month.

Compare that to $4 to $9 for prescription omeprazole with a discount card, and the prescription is almost always cheaper. So why would anyone buy OTC?

When OTC Makes Sense

  • You don't have a prescription. If your doctor hasn't written one and you just need short-term heartburn relief, OTC lets you skip the office visit.
  • You need it today. No waiting for a prescription to be called in or transferred.
  • You only need it occasionally. A 14-day course for occasional heartburn doesn't justify a doctor visit.

When Prescription Is Better

  • You take it daily, long-term. The per-month cost is significantly lower with a prescription and discount card.
  • You have insurance with any drug coverage. OTC purchases don't count toward your deductible. Prescription omeprazole does.
  • You need a higher dose. Prescription omeprazole comes in 40mg. OTC is only 20mg.
Important: OTC omeprazole does not count toward your insurance deductible or out-of-pocket maximum. If you have a high-deductible plan, getting a prescription version — even paying cash with a discount card — may be strategically smarter because those costs can count toward meeting your deductible.

How to Get the Lowest Price on Omeprazole

Follow these steps to make sure you're not overpaying:

1. Ask Your Doctor for a Prescription

Even if you've been buying OTC, ask for a prescription. This opens up the $4 pricing at Walmart and discount card pricing everywhere else. Most doctors will write one without hesitation if you've been taking it regularly.

2. Use a Free Discount Card

GoodRx and SingleCare both offer free coupons that bring omeprazole down to $5 to $9 at most pharmacies. You don't need insurance to use these. Just show the coupon at the pharmacy counter.

3. Check Walmart's $4 List

Prescription omeprazole 20mg is on Walmart's $4 prescription list. A 30-day supply is $4, and a 90-day supply is $10. This is often the cheapest option available, no coupon needed.

4. Consider 90-Day Fills

If you take omeprazole every day, a 90-day fill is almost always cheaper per pill than a 30-day fill. At Walmart, it's $10 for 90 days versus $4 for 30 days — saving you $2 over three months. At other pharmacies, the savings from a 90-day fill can be even bigger.

5. Compare Store-Brand OTC

If you're going the OTC route, skip Prilosec OTC and buy the store brand. Walmart's Equate omeprazole, Costco's Kirkland omeprazole, and Amazon Basic Care omeprazole are all significantly cheaper than the Prilosec label — same drug, same dose.

What About Higher Doses?

If your doctor prescribes omeprazole 40mg, the price goes up slightly but is still affordable. Expect to pay $8 to $15 with a discount card for a 30-day supply. The 40mg strength is not available OTC, so a prescription is the only option at this dose.

Why Omeprazole Prices Vary So Much

A drug that costs $4 at Walmart and $25 at CVS is the same pill in the same bottle. The difference is entirely in pharmacy markup. Chain pharmacies in convenient locations charge more because they can. Warehouse pharmacies like Costco keep margins thin. Walmart uses the $4 list as a loss leader to get you in the door.

This is true for most generic drugs, but it's especially noticeable with something as commonly filled as omeprazole. The lesson is simple: always compare prices before you fill.

When to Talk to Your Doctor

Omeprazole is safe for short-term use, but long-term PPI use (more than a year) has been linked to some concerns including vitamin B12 deficiency, magnesium deficiency, and slightly increased fracture risk. If you've been taking omeprazole for months or years, it's worth discussing with your doctor whether you still need it and whether you can step down to a lower dose or switch to an H2 blocker like famotidine.

None of this means you should stop taking it on your own. Just have the conversation.
