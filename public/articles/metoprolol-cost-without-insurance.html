How Much Does Metoprolol Cost Without Insurance in 2026?

Generic metoprolol often costs $3 to $12 a month with a discount card, compared with $15 to $40 at full retail. Here is what each form costs, how tartrate and succinate differ, and how to pay the lowest price safely.

The Short Answer

Metoprolol is a beta blocker. Doctors prescribe it for high blood pressure, chest pain, heart failure, and some heart rhythm problems. It is one of the most prescribed heart medicines in the United States, and both forms are available as low-cost generics.

Without insurance, average retail prices for a month's supply run about $15 to $40, depending on the form and strength. With a free discount card, many people pay $3 to $12 a month. SingleCare reports metoprolol succinate ER for as little as about $2.85 for a 30-day supply. GoodRx lists most common strengths at about $9 to $12.

Two Forms, Two Price Lists

Metoprolol comes in two main forms. They contain the same active medicine, but they release it differently.

  • Metoprolol tartrate (brand name Lopressor) is immediate-release. Most people take it twice a day.
  • Metoprolol succinate ER (brand name Toprol-XL) is extended-release. Most people take it once a day.

The two forms are not interchangeable milligram for milligram. Your prescriber chooses the form and dose for your condition. Do not switch forms to save money unless your prescriber writes a new prescription.

Brand-Name Prices

Brand-name Toprol-XL and Lopressor cost much more than the generics. SingleCare has listed brand Toprol-XL 50 mg at about $58 for 30 tablets without insurance. There is rarely a medical reason to pay brand prices when your prescriber allows the generic. Kapspargo Sprinkle is a once-daily metoprolol capsule with no generic version, so it costs more. Ask your prescriber whether a standard generic tablet works for you.

Six Ways to Pay Less

  1. Compare prices across pharmacies. The same 30 tablets can cost $3 at one store and $20 at another.
  2. Ask for a 90-day supply. Metoprolol is a long-term medicine for most people. A 90-day fill often lowers the cost per month and cuts trips to the pharmacy.
  3. Compare the coupon price with your copay. For low-cost generics, a discount card can beat an insurance copay. Ask the pharmacist to run both prices.
  4. Check mail-order and online pharmacies. Online pharmacies with transparent pricing can offer low prices on common generics, especially for 90-day supplies.
  5. Ask about the dosing schedule. If you take tartrate twice a day, ask your prescriber whether once-daily succinate ER fits your condition. The change can be simpler and sometimes cheaper. Only your prescriber can make this change.
  6. Check store generic programs. Some retail chains include metoprolol on low-cost generic lists. These lists change, so confirm the current price.

Take Metoprolol Safely

Do Not Stop Suddenly

Metoprolol carries an FDA boxed warning about sudden stops. In people with heart disease, an abrupt stop can worsen chest pain and has led to heart attacks. If cost makes it hard to keep taking metoprolol, talk to your prescriber or pharmacist before you run out. They can help you find a lower price or a safe plan.

Keep Your Supply Steady

Set a refill reminder about a week before you run out. When you change pharmacies to save money, transfer the prescription early so you do not miss doses.

Watch for Interactions

Some medicines raise metoprolol levels or add to its effect on heart rate and blood pressure. Over-the-counter decongestants can also raise blood pressure. Give your pharmacist a complete list of your medicines and supplements, especially if you use more than one pharmacy.

Know the Common Side Effects

Tiredness, dizziness, and a slow heartbeat are common, especially early on. Stand up slowly if you feel lightheaded. Call your prescriber if you feel faint, have trouble breathing, or notice new swelling in your legs.

What Metoprolol Treats

Metoprolol slows the heart rate and reduces the force of each heartbeat. This lowers blood pressure and reduces the heart's need for oxygen. Doctors use it to treat high blood pressure, to prevent chest pain (angina), and to improve survival after a heart attack. The extended-release form also treats some types of heart failure. Some doctors use it for certain heart rhythm problems as well.

Most people take metoprolol for years. That is why a few dollars a month matters. A $10 monthly difference adds up to $120 a year, and many people take two or three heart medicines at once.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is generic metoprolol as good as Toprol-XL?

The FDA requires generic metoprolol to contain the same active medicine as the brand and to work the same way in the body. Some people notice small differences when they change manufacturers. If you feel different after a refill, tell your pharmacist and prescriber.

Can I split metoprolol tablets to save money?

Many metoprolol succinate ER tablets have a score line and can be split in half along that line. Do not crush or chew extended-release tablets. Ask your pharmacist whether your specific tablet can be split, and get your prescriber's approval before you change how you take it.

Does Medicare cover metoprolol?

Nearly all Medicare Part D plans cover generic metoprolol, usually in a low tier. Compare your plan's copay with the cash price on RxGator. For cheap generics, the cash price is sometimes lower.

Why did my metoprolol price change at the same pharmacy?

Generic prices change when a pharmacy switches suppliers, when a discount card changes its contract, or when your insurance changes its tier list. Your price can also change if your prescriber changes the strength or the quantity. If the price jumps, search it again on RxGator before you pay. Another pharmacy may still have the lower price.

How much does a 90-day supply cost?

A 90-day supply of generic metoprolol often costs less per month than three separate 30-day fills. Some pharmacies charge a flat price for 90 tablets that is close to their 30-tablet price. Ask your prescriber to write the prescription for 90 days, then compare the 90-day price at several pharmacies.

What if I cannot afford any of these prices?

Tell your prescriber and pharmacist. Community health centers, state assistance programs, and nonprofit pharmacies can help people with low incomes. Never skip doses or stop metoprolol to save money.

The Bottom Line

Generic metoprolol is one of the cheapest heart medicines you can buy. Most people should pay well under $15 a month, and many pay under $5. Compare pharmacies, ask for a 90-day supply, and check the coupon price against your copay. Never stop metoprolol suddenly. If the price becomes a problem, call your prescriber or pharmacist first.
