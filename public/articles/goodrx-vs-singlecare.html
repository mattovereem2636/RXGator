GoodRx vs SingleCare: Which Saves You More?

Both offer free discount cards, but prices vary wildly by pharmacy and medication. We break down which one wins and when.

GoodRx and SingleCare are both free prescription discount card programs that can dramatically reduce what you pay at the pharmacy. Neither is insurance — they're negotiated discount rates that pharmacies agree to honor. But which one actually saves you more money?

The honest answer: it depends on the drug, the dose, and the pharmacy. Neither wins across the board, which is exactly why comparing both is important.

How They Work

Both programs work the same way at the counter. You search for your medication on their website or app, find a coupon with a price at a nearby pharmacy, and show it to the pharmacist when you pick up your prescription. The pharmacist runs the discount instead of your insurance, and you pay the discounted cash price.

Both are free to use, require no signup or membership, and work at most major pharmacy chains. Neither is insurance, so the amount you pay doesn't count toward your insurance deductible or out-of-pocket maximum.

Where GoodRx Wins

GoodRx typically has a wider network of pharmacy benefit managers behind its coupons, which means it can sometimes offer lower prices at specific pharmacies where SingleCare doesn't have as strong a negotiated rate. GoodRx also tends to have better brand-name drug pricing because of its relationships with more PBMs.

GoodRx's app and website are more established, with features like price alerts, a pill identifier, and telehealth services. Their "GoodRx Gold" membership ($9.99/month) offers additional savings for people who fill multiple prescriptions regularly.

Where SingleCare Wins

SingleCare frequently beats GoodRx on common generic medications at major chains like CVS and Walgreens. Their pricing tends to be more consistent — you're less likely to see the same drug vary by $15 between two locations of the same chain.

SingleCare is also the default savings card at Walmart pharmacies (replacing the old Walmart Rx Savings program in some markets), and they partner with Kroger pharmacies where GoodRx coupons are sometimes not accepted.

When Neither Card Wins

For some medications, both discount cards are beat by other options entirely. Walmart's $4 list doesn't require any card at all. Cost Plus Drugs sells many generics cheaper than either discount card price. Amazon RxPass covers 50+ generics for a flat $5 per month total. And Costco's member pricing on generics is often lower than any coupon.

Discount cards are a great tool, but they're one tool among several. The smartest approach is to compare all your options — insurance copay, GoodRx, SingleCare, Walmart $4, Cost Plus, and any manufacturer programs — before filling. That's a lot of tabs to keep open, which is why we built RxGator.

Important Fine Print

Both programs collect data on your prescription fills. GoodRx shares information with advertisers and has faced FTC scrutiny over its data practices. SingleCare's privacy policy is similar. If data privacy concerns you, read both policies carefully.

Also: discount card prices fluctuate. The price you see online today might not be the exact price at the counter tomorrow. Always confirm the price with the pharmacist before they fill the prescription, and don't be afraid to ask them to try both cards to see which rings up lower.
