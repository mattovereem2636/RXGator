How to Save on Eliquis: Coupons, Alternatives, and What's Coming

Eliquis is one of the most prescribed and most expensive brand-name drugs in America. Whether you are paying out of pocket or struggling with insurance copays, here are your options for bringing the cost down.

The Cost Problem with Eliquis

Eliquis (apixaban), manufactured by Bristol-Myers Squibb (BMS) and Pfizer, is a direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) used to prevent blood clots, stroke in atrial fibrillation patients, and to treat deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism. It is one of the most widely prescribed medications in the United States, with tens of millions of prescriptions filled each year. It is also one of the most expensive.

The list price for Eliquis is over $600 per month for the standard dose of 5 mg twice daily. For patients without insurance, or whose insurance requires a high copay or coinsurance, this cost can be devastating. Eliquis consistently ranks among the top drugs by total spending in both commercial insurance and Medicare, and it is a medication that most patients need to take indefinitely. Unlike a short course of antibiotics, the financial burden of Eliquis is ongoing and cumulative.

The BMS Co-Pay Card: $10 Per Month

The single most impactful savings tool for commercially insured patients is the Eliquis Co-Pay Card offered by Bristol-Myers Squibb. This program can reduce your out-of-pocket cost to as little as $10 per month, regardless of what your insurance plan's copay or coinsurance would otherwise be. The card covers the difference between your plan's cost-sharing and the $10 patient price, up to a maximum annual benefit that typically covers a full year of prescriptions.

There are important eligibility restrictions. The co-pay card is available only to patients with commercial (private) insurance. It is not available to patients on Medicare, Medicaid, Tricare, or other government-funded programs. You must also have insurance that covers Eliquis, even if the copay is high. If your insurance does not cover Eliquis at all, the co-pay card will not help.

To enroll, visit the official Eliquis website or ask your pharmacist. The card can often be activated at the pharmacy counter during your first fill. If you are a commercially insured patient paying more than $10 per month for Eliquis, you should absolutely be using this program.

Key point: The BMS co-pay card can bring your Eliquis cost down to $10/month if you have commercial insurance. There is no reason for commercially insured patients to pay more than this.

Medicare, the Inflation Reduction Act, and What's Changing

For Medicare patients, the Eliquis cost picture is undergoing a historic shift. Eliquis was selected as one of the first ten drugs subject to Medicare price negotiation under the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA). The negotiated price, which took effect in January 2026, represents a significant reduction from the previous list price for Medicare Part D enrollees. The exact savings depend on your specific Part D plan, but the negotiated price is substantially below the previous wholesale acquisition cost.

In addition to the negotiated price, the IRA's $2,000 annual out-of-pocket cap for Medicare Part D means that even patients taking expensive medications like Eliquis will not spend more than $2,000 total per year on all their Part D prescriptions combined. For a patient who was previously spending $200 or more per month on Eliquis copays alone, this cap represents meaningful relief. Medicare patients can also spread their out-of-pocket costs evenly across the year through the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan, which breaks the annual cost into predictable monthly payments.

These changes are significant, but they apply only to Medicare. Patients with commercial insurance or no insurance at all do not benefit from the IRA's negotiated prices directly, though the broader market effects may eventually influence pricing across the board.

The Generic Timeline for Apixaban

The question on every Eliquis patient's mind is when a generic version will be available. BMS and Pfizer have defended their Eliquis patents aggressively, and the patent landscape is complex, with multiple patents covering different aspects of the drug. Patent settlements with generic manufacturers have established a timeline for generic apixaban entry, with the first generics expected to reach the market in late 2026 or 2027, depending on the outcome of ongoing legal proceedings.

When generic apixaban does arrive, it will likely follow the pattern of other blockbuster drugs that have gone generic: prices will drop dramatically, often by 80% or more within a few years of generic entry. A medication that costs $600 per month as a brand could cost $30 to $60 per month as a generic. This is the single biggest price reduction that Eliquis patients can look forward to, but it requires patience.

Alternatives to Eliquis and Why Switching Is Not Simple

Patients sometimes ask about cheaper alternatives to Eliquis. The oldest and cheapest anticoagulant is warfarin, which costs as little as $4 per month as a generic. Warfarin is effective, but it comes with significant lifestyle trade-offs: it requires regular blood testing (INR monitoring), interacts with many foods (especially those high in vitamin K), and interacts with numerous other medications. Eliquis was developed specifically to avoid these inconveniences, and for most patients, the ease of use justifies the higher cost.

Other DOACs like Xarelto (rivarelbaban) and Pradaxa (dabigatran) are in a similar price range as Eliquis and offer their own savings programs. Switching between anticoagulants is not as simple as switching between, say, different blood pressure medications. Anticoagulants affect your blood's ability to clot, and changes to your regimen need to be managed carefully by your doctor to avoid dangerous gaps in protection or excessive bleeding risk. Never stop or switch an anticoagulant without your doctor's direct involvement.

Watch out: Stopping or switching anticoagulants without medical supervision can be dangerous. Even if you find a cheaper alternative, always work with your doctor to make any change safely.

Finding the Best Price Today

While you wait for generics, use every available tool to reduce your current cost. If you have commercial insurance, activate the BMS co-pay card immediately. If you are on Medicare, check whether the IRA negotiated price is reflected in your Part D plan's formulary. If you are uninsured, contact BMS about their patient assistance program, which provides Eliquis at no cost to qualifying low-income patients.
