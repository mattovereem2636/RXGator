Rx Outreach: The Nonprofit Pharmacy Most People Don't Know About

By RXGator · Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

If you're paying more than $20 a month for a common generic medication, you may be overpaying — and the solution isn't another discount card. It's a nonprofit pharmacy called Rx Outreach that ships medications for free to all 50 states, charges transparent prices with no hidden fees, and doesn't require insurance, memberships, or income verification.

Most people have never heard of Rx Outreach. That's a problem, because for many of the most commonly prescribed generic medications, Rx Outreach's prices are the lowest available anywhere — lower than GoodRx coupons, lower than Cost Plus Drugs, and dramatically lower than retail pharmacy prices.

Bottom line: Rx Outreach is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit mail-order pharmacy offering 1,000+ medication strengths with free shipping. No insurance needed. No membership. No income requirements. For many common generics, it's the cheapest option in the country.

What Is Rx Outreach?

Rx Outreach is a fully licensed, NABP-accredited nonprofit mail-order pharmacy headquartered in Maryland Heights, Missouri. Since 2010, they've dispensed over 4 million prescriptions to more than 650,000 patients, saving consumers over $1 billion compared to retail pharmacy prices.

Unlike discount card programs like GoodRx or SingleCare, Rx Outreach is an actual pharmacy. They dispense medications directly, shipped to your door via FedEx. Their pricing is set by the nonprofit — not by pharmacy benefit managers, not by insurance negotiations, not by market dynamics.

How Does It Work?

The process is straightforward:

Step 1: Browse the medication list at rxoutreach.org to check if your drug is available and see the price upfront.

Step 2: Ask your doctor to send your prescription to Rx Outreach, or request a transfer from your current pharmacy.

Step 3: Place your order online or by phone. Pay the listed price — no hidden fees, no surprise charges.

Step 4: Your medication ships free via FedEx. Typical delivery is 5–7 business days.

Good to know: Rx Outreach does not bill insurance. You pay their listed price directly. For many patients, the Rx Outreach price is lower than their insurance copay — which means it's worth checking even if you have coverage.

GoodRx has a larger formulary and lets you pick up at a local pharmacy. Cost Plus Drugs has transparent pricing but charges $5 shipping. Rx Outreach's advantage is the combination of nonprofit pricing and free shipping — for medications they carry, the total cost is often the lowest available.

Who Should Use Rx Outreach?

Rx Outreach is worth checking if you take any generic medication for a chronic condition — especially blood pressure, cholesterol, diabetes, depression, thyroid, or pain management drugs. It's particularly valuable for people who are uninsured, underinsured, or whose insurance copay is higher than the Rx Outreach price (which happens more often than you'd think).

It's not the right fit if you need a medication urgently (mail-order takes 5–7 days), if your drug isn't in their formulary (they focus on generics for chronic conditions), or if you prefer picking up at a local pharmacy.

Is Rx Outreach Legitimate?

Yes. Rx Outreach is accredited by the National Association of Boards of Pharmacy (NABP), holds a 5-star rating on Trustpilot from over 400 reviews, is a verified nonprofit on GuideStar, and partners with the National Association of Free and Charitable Clinics. They purchase medications directly from manufacturers and ship via FedEx.

The Bottom Line

Rx Outreach is one of the best-kept secrets in prescription drug pricing. For many common generics, it offers the lowest total cost in the country — and it does so as a nonprofit, with free shipping, no insurance requirements, and no hidden fees. The only catch is that it's mail-order only, so you'll need to plan ahead rather than picking up same-day at a local pharmacy.

Before you fill your next prescription, take 30 seconds to check whether Rx Outreach carries your medication. If they do, compare the price against what you're currently paying. You might be surprised.
