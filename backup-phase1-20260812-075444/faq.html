Getting Started

Is RXGator really free?

Yes, completely free. There is no charge to search, no account to create, no credit card required, and no premium tier. RXGator is a public-benefit project funded by community support through Patreon and Ko-fi. It will always be free to use.

Why should I use RXGator instead of GoodRx?

GoodRx only shows GoodRx prices. SingleCare only shows SingleCare prices. Walmart doesn't tell you about Cost Plus. Your pharmacist doesn't know about Amazon RxPass. No single source is cheapest for every drug at every pharmacy.

RXGator searches all of them simultaneously — 18 data sources in one click — and shows you which one actually has the best deal. We're not replacing GoodRx. We're showing you whether GoodRx is the best deal, or whether something else beats it.

Sometimes GoodRx wins. Sometimes Walmart's $4 program wins. Sometimes Cost Plus by mail wins. The point is: you shouldn't have to check each one individually. That's what RXGator does for you.

How does RXGator make money?

Transparency matters, so here's exactly how RXGator is funded:

  • Community support — monthly contributions through Patreon and one-time tips through Ko-fi
  • Display advertising — Google AdSense (pending approval) will show contextual ads that are clearly labeled. Your drug search data is never shared with advertisers.
  • Affiliate commissions — when you click through to a pharmacy platform like GoodRx or Cost Plus Drugs and fill a prescription, RXGator may earn a small referral fee. This never affects how results are ranked — cheapest is always first.

We will never sell your search data. If all funding stopped tomorrow, the free government data sources (NADAC, Medicare Part D, RxNorm, MedlinePlus, openFDA) would keep working indefinitely — those cost nothing to query. The tool would still be useful; we just couldn't add new discount card data.

Do I need to create an account?

No. Just go to rxgator.info/app, type your drug name, and search. No registration, no login, no email required.

Is my information private?

Yes. RXGator never sells, shares, or transmits your search data to advertisers or third parties. We don't track you across the web, we don't build profiles, and we don't use cookies for advertising. Your drug searches are logged only to help us understand which drugs are most searched so we can prioritize adding data. Read our full Privacy Policy for details.

What drugs can I search for?

You can search for any prescription drug by generic name (like "atorvastatin") or brand name (like "Lipitor"). RXGator automatically resolves brand names to their generic equivalents and shows you the generic prices, which are almost always cheaper.

Tip: If you're not sure of the generic name, just type the brand name on your prescription bottle. RXGator will find it.
Why does it ask for my zip code?

The zip code field is optional. When provided, it helps identify pharmacy prices near your location. Some data sources like SingleCare and GoodRx offer pharmacy-specific pricing that varies by zip code. If you leave it blank, RXGator shows national pricing from all sources.

We never track or store your location. The zip code is used only for that one search and is not saved to any database.

Understanding Your Results

How do I read the price results?

After searching, you'll see a list of prices sorted lowest to highest. The cheapest option is always first, highlighted in green. Each result shows:

  • The source — where this price comes from (Cost Plus Drugs, GoodRx, Walmart, etc.)
  • The price — what you would pay for a 30-day or 90-day supply
  • "How do I get this price?" — click this for step-by-step instructions on how to actually get the price at the pharmacy

The first result's instructions are already expanded so you can see exactly what to do.

Are these prices guaranteed?

Prices are estimates. Drug prices can change daily, and the actual price at the pharmacy may differ based on your location, the specific pharmacy, and current availability. Always verify the price at the pharmacy counter before filling your prescription.

That said, the prices shown are based on real data from government databases, pharmacy programs, and discount card platforms. They are the best available estimates at the time of your search.

What is the "Pharmacy Cost" benchmark?

The Pharmacy Cost (also called NADAC) is what your pharmacy pays to buy the drug from the manufacturer. It's published by the government as a transparency measure. If the price you're being charged is much higher than this number, there's a significant markup — and you should ask your pharmacist about cheaper options or use a discount card.

What is the "Medicare Average Cost"?

This shows the average cost per prescription fill across all Medicare Part D plans. It's useful as a benchmark — if you're on Medicare and paying significantly more than this average, you may want to review your plan's formulary or talk to your plan about alternatives.

The trend line shows whether this drug is getting more or less expensive over time (2020–2023 data from CMS).

Saving Money on Prescriptions

How do I use a GoodRx coupon at the pharmacy?

It's simple:

  • Search your drug on RXGator and find the GoodRx price
  • Click "View on GoodRx" in the search results
  • On GoodRx's website, select your pharmacy and get the coupon
  • Show the coupon to your pharmacist (on your phone screen or printed)
  • The pharmacist enters the discount code and you pay the lower price
Important: GoodRx coupons are NOT insurance. They are a separate discount. You can use them even if you have insurance — sometimes the GoodRx price is lower than your insurance copay.
Will my pharmacist accept these prices?

Yes, in most cases. Discount card prices (GoodRx, SingleCare) are accepted at most major pharmacies including CVS, Walgreens, Walmart, Kroger, and Costco. The pharmacist processes the discount as a separate program — you simply show them the coupon or discount card information.

Pharmacy programs like Walmart's $4 program and Cost Plus Drugs are the pharmacy's own pricing — just ask for them at the counter.

If a pharmacist says they don't accept a particular discount, try a different pharmacy. Acceptance can vary by location.

Can I use these prices with my insurance?

It depends — but sometimes the discount price is cheaper than your insurance copay. Here's how to think about it:

  • If your insurance copay is higher than the discount card price, use the discount card instead. You can ask the pharmacist to run it both ways.
  • If your insurance copay is lower, use your insurance.
  • Discount card prices do NOT count toward your insurance deductible. If you're close to meeting your deductible, using insurance may be better long-term even if the copay is slightly higher.
Tip: Ask your pharmacist: "Can you check if the cash price or a discount card is cheaper than my copay?" They can run it both ways at the counter.
How do I save money if I'm on Medicare?

Medicare Part D covers prescription drugs, but copays can still be significant. Here are your options:

  • Compare your Medicare copay to discount card prices. For some generics, a GoodRx or SingleCare coupon may be cheaper than your Medicare copay.
  • Check if your drug is on the $4 list. Walmart's $4 program and similar pharmacy programs don't require insurance and may beat your copay.
  • Apply for Extra Help (LIS). If your income is limited, Medicare's Extra Help program can reduce drug costs to $4–$10 per prescription. Call 1-800-MEDICARE.
  • Check manufacturer assistance programs. After every RXGator search, you'll see links to NeedyMeds and RxAssist — many manufacturers offer copay assistance even for Medicare beneficiaries.

Important: If you use a discount card instead of Medicare, that cost does NOT count toward your Medicare Part D out-of-pocket maximum.

What are patient assistance programs?

Many drug manufacturers offer programs that provide medications for free or at very low cost to people who qualify. These programs are especially helpful for expensive brand-name drugs.

You may qualify even if you have insurance or Medicare. Eligibility is usually based on income and insurance status. After every RXGator search, you'll see links to:

  • NeedyMeds — searches manufacturer programs by drug name
  • RxAssist — comprehensive database of patient assistance programs

These are free services. Applying typically involves filling out a form from the manufacturer. Your doctor's office can often help with the paperwork.

What's the difference between brand and generic drugs?

A generic drug contains the same active ingredient, in the same dosage and form, as the brand-name drug. The FDA requires generics to be "bioequivalent" — meaning they work the same way in your body.

The difference is price. A brand-name drug like Lipitor might cost $200/month, while generic atorvastatin costs $2–$5/month. Same drug. Same effect. Fraction of the price.

When you search a brand name on RXGator, it automatically shows you generic prices and explains the connection. Over 90% of prescriptions filled in the U.S. are generics.

Important: Always talk to your doctor before switching between brand and generic. In rare cases (certain thyroid medications, anti-seizure drugs), your doctor may have a specific reason for prescribing the brand name.

Medicare Coverage Gap

What is the Medicare "donut hole" and how does RXGator help?

The Medicare Part D "donut hole" (officially the "coverage gap") is a period where your drug costs suddenly increase. Here's how it works:

  • Initial Coverage: You pay your normal copays until your total drug costs reach a threshold (around $5,030 in 2026).
  • Coverage Gap (Donut Hole): You now pay 25% of the cost for brand-name drugs and generics until your out-of-pocket spending reaches the catastrophic threshold.
  • Catastrophic Coverage: Your costs drop significantly — you pay a small copay or coinsurance for the rest of the year.

This is exactly when RXGator is most valuable. When you enter the donut hole, your Medicare costs spike — but discount card prices don't change. A drug that cost you $10/month with your plan copay might suddenly cost $50/month in the gap. Meanwhile, a free GoodRx coupon still gives you the same $2 price it always did.

Donut Hole Strategy: When you hit the coverage gap, search every medication on RXGator and compare the discount card price to your plan's gap pricing. For many generics, paying cash with a discount card is dramatically cheaper than your 25% cost-sharing. Important: discount card purchases do NOT count toward your out-of-pocket maximum, so weigh this decision carefully for expensive drugs.

No Insurance?

I don't have insurance — can RXGator still help me?

Absolutely — RXGator may be even more valuable for you than for insured patients.

Here's what most uninsured people don't realize: discount cards like GoodRx and SingleCare are NOT insurance. They're free discount programs that anyone can use, and they often provide prices lower than what insured patients pay in copays. You don't need to qualify, sign up, or pay anything.

Your playbook as an uninsured patient:

  • Search every drug on RXGator — you'll see prices from 18 sources. The lowest price is what you pay at the pharmacy with a free coupon.
  • Check Walmart's $4 program — 85 generics, $4 for 30 days, no card or coupon needed. Just ask at the counter.
  • Consider Cost Plus Drugs — Mark Cuban's mail-order pharmacy charges cost + 15% + flat fees. Often the cheapest option.
  • Amazon RxPass — $5/month covers 55+ generics if you have Amazon Prime.
  • Apply for manufacturer assistance — RXGator links to NeedyMeds and RxAssist after every search. Many manufacturers provide expensive medications for free to uninsured patients.
  • Check Medicaid eligibility — visit healthcare.gov/medicaid-chip to see if you qualify.
Key insight: For common generics, an uninsured person using a free GoodRx coupon often pays LESS than an insured person paying their copay. The system is that broken. RXGator exists to show you all your options.

PrEP & High-Cost Specialty Medications

I take PrEP (Truvada or Descovy) — can RXGator help?

PrEP costs $1,600-2,000/month without assistance. But most people qualify for programs that reduce the cost to $0.

  • Gilead Advancing Access Copay Card — commercial insurance? Gilead reduces your cost to $0. Apply at gileadadvancingaccess.com
  • Gilead Patient Assistance — uninsured? Free medication. Same website.
  • Ready, Set, PrEP — federal program providing PrEP at no cost. Visit readysetprep.hiv.gov
  • State ADAP programs — many states cover PrEP through AIDS Drug Assistance Programs.
Nobody should go without PrEP because of cost. Between Gilead's programs and Ready, Set, PrEP, $0 PrEP is available to virtually everyone who needs it.

Community Health Centers & 340B Program

What is the 340B program? Could I already be getting cheaper drugs?

The 340B Drug Pricing Program requires manufacturers to sell medications at deep discounts to community health centers, public hospitals, and certain clinics. If you receive care at one of these facilities, you may have access to prescriptions at $0-5 per fill.

You might qualify if you are uninsured, underinsured, have low income, or receive care at a federally funded clinic.

Find a health center near you: findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov — enter your zip code. These centers serve everyone regardless of ability to pay.

Already at a community health center? Ask if they have a 340B pharmacy. You may already be getting the best possible price.
