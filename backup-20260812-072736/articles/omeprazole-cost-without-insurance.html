How Much Does Omeprazole Cost Without Insurance in 2026?

Omeprazole is one of the ten most prescribed medications in America, and it is also available over the counter. That creates an unusual pricing situation — here is how to figure out which version costs less and where to find the best deal.

What Omeprazole Actually Costs at the Pharmacy

Omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) used to treat gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), stomach ulcers, and conditions where the stomach produces too much acid. It works by reducing the amount of acid your stomach makes, and it has been available as a generic since the early 2000s. What makes omeprazole unique among common generics is that it is available both as a prescription medication and over the counter (OTC), and the two versions are priced very differently.

For the prescription version, walking into a pharmacy without insurance and paying the full retail cash price can mean $60 to $95 for a 30-day supply of 20 mg or 40 mg capsules. That number shocks people, especially since the wholesale cost per capsule is around $0.07. The markup from wholesale to retail is where pharmacies make their margin, and on prescription omeprazole, that margin can be substantial.

With a free discount card from GoodRx, SingleCare, or RxSaver, the prescription version drops dramatically — typically to $5 to $15 for a 30-day supply at major chain pharmacies. That is a savings of $50 to $80 compared to the retail cash price, and it is available to anyone regardless of insurance status.

Key point: Prescription omeprazole can cost $90 at retail or $5 with a discount card — at the same pharmacy. The card is free. There is no reason not to use one.

Prescription vs. Over-the-Counter: Which Is Cheaper?

This is the question most people have, and the answer depends on how you are paying. OTC omeprazole (sold as Prilosec OTC, Omeprazole DR, and various store brands) is available at any pharmacy, grocery store, or big-box retailer without a prescription. A 14-day supply of the OTC 20 mg delayed-release tablets typically costs $8 to $14, and a 42-count box (three 14-day courses) runs $18 to $28.

If you need omeprazole daily for longer than 14 days, the OTC version gets expensive fast. A full 30-day supply at OTC prices works out to roughly $17 to $30 per month, depending on the brand and where you buy it. Store brands (Walmart's Equate, Costco's Kirkland, Target's Up & Up) are significantly cheaper than name-brand Prilosec OTC.

Here is the math that matters: prescription generic omeprazole with a discount card typically costs $5 to $15 for a 30-day supply. OTC omeprazole for 30 days costs $17 to $30. For ongoing daily use, the prescription version with a discount card is almost always cheaper than buying it off the shelf. The catch is that you need a prescription from your doctor, which means a visit or at least a telehealth appointment.

For occasional use — a 14-day course to treat a flare-up — OTC is more convenient and cost-effective since you avoid the doctor visit entirely.

Generic Omeprazole vs. Brand-Name Prilosec

Omeprazole was originally sold as Prilosec by AstraZeneca. The brand-name prescription version can cost $400 or more for a 30-day supply, making it one of the most overpriced brand-name generics on the market. There is absolutely no clinical reason to fill the brand version. Generic omeprazole is the same active ingredient, same dosage, same FDA-regulated quality standards.

On the OTC side, brand-name Prilosec OTC costs about twice as much as store-brand omeprazole OTC. A 42-count box of Prilosec OTC runs $24 to $28, while the Costco Kirkland brand or Walmart Equate equivalent costs $10 to $16 for the same quantity. The active ingredient is identical.

Where to Find the Lowest Price

For ongoing daily use with a prescription, the cheapest option is usually a discount card at a major chain pharmacy. GoodRx and SingleCare both show 30-day prices of $5 to $12 at CVS, Walgreens, Kroger, and Walmart. Cost Plus Drugs sells prescription omeprazole at close to wholesale cost, typically $4 to $6 for a 30-day supply plus shipping.

For OTC omeprazole, Costco's Kirkland Signature brand is consistently the best value — a 42-count box (42 days of treatment) runs about $10 to $14, which works out to roughly $0.25 to $0.33 per day. Walmart's Equate brand is similarly priced. Amazon Subscribe & Save can also offer competitive pricing with the convenience of automatic delivery.

For a 90-day supply of prescription omeprazole, discount card prices often drop to $12 to $25 — under $8 per month. If your doctor is willing to write a 90-day prescription, this is almost certainly the cheapest way to take omeprazole long-term.

Important: OTC omeprazole is labeled for 14-day courses, repeated no more than every 4 months. If you need it daily for longer, talk to your doctor about a prescription — it will likely be cheaper and your doctor can monitor for long-term side effects.

Insurance Copays vs. Cash Price

The insurance picture for omeprazole is complicated by the OTC availability. Some insurance plans do not cover prescription omeprazole at all because an OTC version exists, or they require step therapy (trying the OTC version first). Other plans cover it on Tier 1 with a $5 to $15 copay.

If your plan does cover it, compare the copay to the discount card price. If your copay is $10 and the discount card price is $5, cash wins. But remember that copays count toward your deductible and out-of-pocket max, while cash payments do not. For a drug this inexpensive, the difference is usually minor.

If your insurance refuses to cover prescription omeprazole, a discount card effectively eliminates the problem. You get the prescription filled at a lower price than the OTC version costs, without needing insurance approval at all.
